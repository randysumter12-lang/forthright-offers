Section 03

What Happens Next

Your comment is now part of the official administrative record. Here is what the federal rulemaking process looks like from this point forward:

  • 1

    Agency Review of All Public Comments. The issuing agency is required to review and consider every public comment submitted during the open period. Comments from organizations with demonstrated regulatory standing — like yours — carry significant weight in the agency's deliberative process.

  • 2

    Final Rule Published in the Federal Register. After completing its review, the agency issues a final rule in the Federal Register. The final rule must respond to substantive comments, making your on-record position a matter of public documentation.

  • 3

    Comment Period Typically Spans 30–90 Days. Federal comment windows commonly run 30, 60, or 90 days from the date of the proposed rule's Federal Register publication. Time from comment submission to final rule can range from several months to over a year, depending on rule complexity and agency workload.

  • 4

    Organizations May Submit Reply Comments. In some rulemakings, a supplemental or reply comment period is opened after initial comments are published. If applicable, your organization may have an additional opportunity to respond to other stakeholders' positions before the final rule is issued.
